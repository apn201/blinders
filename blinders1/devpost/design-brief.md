# Design brief: Blinders

<!-- Filled in by plan-lens from the learner's interview. Use the learner's words. Mark any value they didn't choose as (default). -->

## The one spatial moment
An operator in safety glasses walks up to a warehouse rack. The glasses read the shelf label and the box labels, check them against the ERP, and stay quiet unless something is wrong ("WH01 Shelf A-01-01 - Seems empty. Should have 1 box of 100201 (Grapnel line 50 m), 1 box of 100701 (Latex gloves L)"). Then a person walks into view: the camera stops, a small "PERSON DETECTED · CAMERA PAUSED" appears, and the log shows exactly what was sent where and when it paused. The ERP audit is the story; the product is a camera that can prove it only looks at barcodes.

## Why it belongs on glasses
- Uses: shares your view (reads the labels you're looking at), draws in the world (callouts on the shelf and box labels), leaves your body free (hands stay on the work, eyes stay up in a forklift environment).
- Why it's better than the phone or laptop version: a handheld scanner stops the work and needs hands. Glasses do the check while you walk, but only if a security director can trust what the camera sees. Proving that trust is the point.
- Real-product direction (not in this demo): built into the safety glasses operators already wear; read warning signs, warn on entering restricted areas, forklifts, hazardous goods; never record people or sensitive things.

## Product or overlay
- Type: overlay. It responds to real shelf labels, box labels and people.
- What the real version would sense: shelf barcodes (`BIN:WH01:...`), box SSCC labels, people, and later ID cards and computer screens.
- Stand-in for the preview:
  - **Barcode read:** a label counts as read when the operator looks straight at it (ray from the centre of view hits the label, within about 2.5 m) (default). Shelves, boxes and labels are virtual, at real size, with barcode/QR-looking labels.
  - **ERP:** a local copy of `../data/erp.json` (generated by the learner's ERP mockup) bundled with the Lens. Use its bins, materials, SSCCs, best-before dates and actions; the json is the source of truth for all names and messages. No network. The Lens must survive any mismatch between what it sees and what the ERP says (unknown codes, missing or extra boxes, empty shelves): finding mismatches is what it is for, so it reports them, never crashes or hangs.
  - **Person:** a simple virtual figure that walks between the operator and the racks on a loop (about every 20 s, stopping in front of the racks for about 8 s) (default). When it is in view, the Lens treats it as "person detected".

## Where things live
- Where the user is looking during the main task: straight ahead at the rack, about 1 to 2 m away.
- Racks: two racks, A and B, three levels each, real size (about 120 cm wide, 180 cm tall, 50 cm deep) (default), about 1.5 m in front of the start position, contents per `erp.json`. Shelf A-01-01 is empty so the "Seems empty" case shows.
- Shelf-label callout: a small callout coming from the shelf label, a few cm above it. Shows the shelf ID and, in mode 1, the problem below it.
- Box callouts: small callouts from each box label, a few cm above it. ID and problem (mode 1); ID, batch, best-before etc. (mode 2).
- Text: short, light colour, max 3 lines per callout, letters about 1.5 to 2 cm tall so they read at 1 to 2 m (default). No dark plates (additive display).
- "PERSON DETECTED · CAMERA PAUSED": a small pill a little below eye level, about 1 m away, gently following the head (default). The learner asked for "bottom of the screen"; this is the comfortable version of that. The only thing that follows the head (besides the mode menu while open).
- Mode menu: appears about 1 m ahead, a little below eye level, three big items: 0, 1, 2.
- Audit log: not in the scene. Printed verbatim to Lens Studio's Logger panel (stand-in for the plain .txt log on a server).

## Hands and input
- Open the mode menu: look down (about 60° below the horizon) for 2 seconds; a ring fills as a timer. (Changed from 4 s after the first build: 4 s felt long; 60° keeps a close look at the bottom shelf from opening it.)
- Pick a mode: keep the gaze cursor on an item; a circle fills as a visual timer and locks it in (about 1.5 s) (default). Pinching an item also works (so the mouse can drive it in the preview).
- Close the menu: after choosing, or by looking away from it for about 3 s (default).
- Targets are big and highlight when aimed at.
- Voice: none.

## When things show
- **Mode 0 - normal glasses:** nothing shows, nothing is read, camera off. Logged once: mode 0, camera off.
- **Mode 1 - problems only:** looking at a shelf reads the shelf label, queries the ERP for what should be there, then looks for those box labels. If everything matches, nothing is shown. Problems show as callouts: empty shelf vs ERP ("WH01 Shelf A-01-01 - Seems empty. Should have 1 box of 100201 (Grapnel line 50 m), 1 box of 100701 (Latex gloves L)"), expired batch, wrong shelf, count mismatch, open ERP action.
- **Mode 2 - informative:** reads all barcodes and shows callouts on all shelves and items, all warnings, plus soft prompts like "Confirm that XYZ is in shelf A-01-01". Labels that match the ERP get a small green dot (SPEC R4), only in mode 2; mode 1 shows nothing for them. Mode 2 is the "show everything" layer for the video; mode 1 is what an operator would live in, so it must never overwhelm.
- Clutter: older callouts fade out. A callout fades after about 5 s once you stop looking at that shelf (default); only shelves within about 2.5 m get callouts (default).
- **Person detected (modes 1 and 2):** camera pauses immediately, no reads run, all callouts hide, the pause pill shows. After 5 s, the Lens takes one test frame: person still there, stay paused and log it; gone, resume and log it.
- If something needs attention out of view: none. The operator only gets information about what they are looking at.

### Audit log (Logger panel)
Every event as one plain line with a timestamp, verbatim, e.g.:
- `14:02:10 mode 1 selected`
- `14:02:11 read BIN:WH01:A-01-01 -> ERP query (local copy)`
- `14:02:11 ERP reply: A-01-01 expects 1x 100201, 1x 100701`
- `14:02:13 A-01-01 seen 0 boxes -> warning "Seems empty"`
- `14:02:15 person detected -> camera PAUSED, 0 frames sent`
- `14:02:20 recheck: 1 frame -> person still present, stay paused`
- `14:02:25 recheck: 1 frame -> clear, camera RESUMED`
Never log anything other than the allow-listed shelf/box codes and these events.

## Done criteria
Each one can be checked in Lens Studio's preview.
1. In mode 0, looking at the racks shows nothing, and the Logger shows the camera is off and no reads happen.
2. In mode 1, looking at shelf A-01-01 shows the callout "WH01 Shelf A-01-01 - Seems empty. Should have 1 box of 100201 (Grapnel line 50 m), 1 box of 100701 (Latex gloves L)", and shelves that match the ERP show nothing.
3. In mode 2, looking at the racks shows callouts on all shelf and box labels with all warnings and green dots on matching labels, and callouts for shelves you've looked away from fade out after about 5 s.
4. When the virtual person walks into view, callouts disappear, "PERSON DETECTED · CAMERA PAUSED" shows, and the Logger shows the pause; 5 s later a single recheck is logged, and the camera resumes (and logs it) once the person has gone.
5. Looking down for 2 s opens the mode menu; holding the gaze on an item fills the timer circle and switches the mode (pinch also works).

## Out of scope
- Real barcode reading, real person detection, ID card and computer screen detection (person only in this demo).
- Writing the log to a server or file (Logger panel stands in).
- Live ERP connection or any network use.
- Mode 3 (extra detail level).
- Warning signs, restricted areas, forklifts, hazardous goods.
- Pointing at a box for detail, highlighting the physical box.

## Risks and open questions
- Gaze-dwell and look-down-to-open are untested for comfort; looking down for 2 s while walking near forklifts may be awkward. Feel unproven.
- Whether real on-glass person detection is fast and reliable enough to pause before a frame is processed: only real glasses can tell.
- Callouts on every label in mode 2 may still clutter a full rack; the fade timing needs tuning.
- The ERP data in the Lens is a bundled copy; it must be refreshed from the mockup when the mockup data changes.

## Build notes for CLAD
- Build for SPECS so it runs and can be demonstrated in Lens Studio's preview. No SPECS device is available.
- Use Spectacles Interaction Kit (SIK) and UI Kit for all interactions, so they work with the mouse in Interactive Preview and with LEAF.
- Build any stand-in exactly as described above, and keep it obvious in the scene.
- Use real-world scale in centimeters. Keep content inside the user's view during the main task.
- The display is additive: black renders as transparent. Don't rely on black or very dark content to be seen; keep text light and readable from where the user stands.
- Get the basic flow working first, then add detail.
- Write LEAF scenarios for each done criterion, run them in preview, and report which pass. Say which criteria have no test.
- Don't record, identify, or follow people.
- Project-specific: the learner's `../docs/SPEC.md` holds the full check rules (R1-R7), label formats (P4) and privacy invariants (P1-P3: no network module of any kind, no reads while paused, nothing persisted). Follow it where it doesn't conflict with this brief; this brief wins on modes, callouts and the pause flow.

## After the build: judge it
This part is for you, after CLAD finishes.

1. **Does it work?** Ask your agent which LEAF tests passed and which done criteria have no test.
2. **Walk around it.** Open the preview in Interactive Preview. Use WASD to move, Q and E to go down and up, and right-drag (or hold Shift) to look around. Clicking acts as a pinch. Stand where a user would and look at the task.
3. **Ask the three questions.**
   - Does it use what I'm looking at?
   - Does it put something in my space, where it belongs?
   - Does it free my hands or let me keep my eyes up?
   - Would it be just as good on a phone? If so, what would make it need the glasses?
4. **Check comfort.**
   - Everything I need is in view while I do the task. Nothing sits at the edges, behind me, or stuck to my face.
   - Every control is within reach of someone standing there.
   - Text reads easily from where I stand. No dark UI disappearing.
   - Things show when needed and get out of the way.
5. **Label what you know.** Write "logic proven" next to anything the tests and preview show working, and "feel unproven" next to anything only real glasses could tell you (comfort over time, real hand tracking, real recognition).
6. **Fix with design notes, not code.** Tell your agent what to change in design terms, for example: "Move the step card 15 cm lower and next to the pan. Make the Next button twice as big. Re-run the tests." If a fix takes under a minute, like nudging a panel, do it yourself in the scene.
7. **Submit.** Record a video under 3 minutes of your Lens in the preview, public on YouTube. Make your repo public with your Lens Studio project and this `devpost/` folder. Details are on the hackathon site: https://learn-specs.devpost.com/resources
